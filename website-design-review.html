Session 1

A design read on kurajewellery.com.

Walked the homepage, collections, a product detail page, and the Our Story page. The product range is genuinely luxury but the site reads at a softer tier than the pieces deserve. Here's what's working, what's working against you, and where to focus first.

What's working.

  • Hero photography is good. The animated GIF treatment communicates motion and modernity.
  • Generous whitespace in the grid. The product photos breathe instead of crowding each other.
  • Mega-menu structure is sophisticated and matches the depth of the catalog.
  • Product detail pages tell a story. The cage etymology, the pricing philosophy, the materials breakdown — none of that is generic Shopify copy. Keep it.
  • Six product photos per piece with multiple angles. This is well above the boutique average.
  • Judge.me reviews are embedded on product pages. Real customer voice, named reviewers, 100% 5-star on the Signature Cage.

What's holding the premium feel back.

Typography on internal pages reads like a worksheet.

The Our Story page uses headers like "WHO ? WHEN ? WHERE ?" and "WHAT IS IT ABOUT ?" — all caps, space before the question mark, no hierarchy variation. It's the single biggest tell that this is a small-business site, not a luxury house. Cartier, Mejuri, Catbird all use mixed-case editorial headers with a serif/sans pairing. Replace these with phrases like "Founded in Hong Kong, 2009", "The pricing philosophy", "Where each piece is made".

The hero says "Discover a world of Kura Jewellery" — but doesn't say what kind of world.

Brand-name-as-headline is what big houses do because their brand already does the work (Cartier, Bvlgari). For a 2,400-follower brand, the hero needs to teach the visitor what makes Kura different in the first 3 seconds. Something like "Fine jewellery, fairly priced. Crafted in Italy and Hong Kong." uses the anti-markup ethos and the artisan story at the same time. It's also a better SEO signal.

Erny is invisible on the homepage.

Her story is the strongest moat the brand has — banking VP, GIA-trained, lived in eight cities, anti-markup ethos. None of that surfaces until someone clicks Our Story (which most won't). At minimum, a small "From the founder" panel on the homepage with her photo and a 2-line quote pulls the brand from "Shopify store" to "person you can trust." Mejuri and Aurate both do this within the first scroll.

The product page price comparison reads as discount-store, not luxury.

Lines like "$2,250.00 — Estimated Retail Price Elsewhere: $5,625.00" are how Marshall's frames a deal, not how an 18K gold brand should frame fair pricing. The anti-markup story is real and worth telling, but the framing should be confident, not comparative. Try: "$2,250 — priced at fair multiple of materials and craft, not industry markup." Same number, completely different read.

The collection grid is dense, premium uses negative space.

Five products per row at the desktop breakpoint pushes each piece into a thumbnail. Luxury houses sit at 3 per row, sometimes 2 for hero collections. The math: bigger images, fewer competing CTAs in the viewport, longer dwell time per piece. The whole catalog can stay accessible behind one more scroll.

The pricing philosophy chart on Our Story is invisible.

The anti-markup philosophy is THE strongest differentiator and it's buried in a chart on the Our Story page. This deserves a dedicated panel on the homepage and a permanent footer line ("Fair pricing, not industry markup"). Right now it's a footnote.

No founder face means no founder voice.

The site has a great photo of Erny on the Our Story page. Pull it onto the homepage, into product detail pages ("a note from Erny"), and into the bespoke booking flow. Buyers paying $1,000-$5,000 for a piece want to know who designed it. Right now they have to dig.

Hero animated GIF is a performance and accessibility risk.

GIFs are 10-100x larger than equivalent MP4 or WebM. On mobile, that's a noticeable load time hit. Convert to inline autoplay MP4/WebM with playsinline muted attributes. Same visual, fraction of the bandwidth. Also add prefers-reduced-motion handling so viewers who've turned off motion get a still image instead.

Quick wins (one afternoon each).

  • Rewrite Our Story headers from "WHO ? WHEN ? WHERE ?" to proper editorial headlines
  • Add a "From the founder" panel to the homepage with Erny's photo and a 2-line quote
  • Reframe product-page price-comparison copy from "Estimated Retail Price Elsewhere" to a confident fair-pricing line
  • Convert hero GIF to MP4/WebM (any developer can do this in under an hour)
  • Add a permanent footer line: "Fair pricing, not industry markup. Crafted in Italy and Hong Kong since 2009."

Bigger projects (worth scoping with a designer).

  • Drop the collection grid from 5-up to 3-up at desktop and revisit product-card type
  • Build a dedicated "Pricing philosophy" page (not buried in Our Story) and link it from every product page
  • Redesign the Our Story page as an editorial spread, not a worksheet — full-width images, generous serif headers, a real beginning-middle-end narrative
  • Add a "Meet Erny" / "Book a consultation" funnel that runs hero → founder bio → bespoke booking, separate from the standard shop funnel

The single highest-leverage change.

If you only do one thing: put Erny on the homepage. Photo, name, one-sentence story, link to bespoke. Every other change here is a multiplier on a brand that already feels personal. Without the founder visible, the site reads as one of a thousand Shopify stores. With her visible, it reads as a designer brand with a person behind it. That's the entire competitive moat in 2026.
